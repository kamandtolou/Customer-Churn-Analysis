#Business Insights

## Executive Summary 
This analysis explores customer churn using a dataset of 10,000 bank customers. The goal was to identify the key factors associated with customer churn and provide business recommendations based on data-driven insights. SQL was used for exploratory analysis, Excel for data cleaning, and Tableau for visualization.

## Key Findings 

### 1. Churn by Age 

- Customers aged 50+ have the highest churn rate (44.65%).
- Customers aged 18–30 have the lowest churn rate (7.52%).
- Customers aged 50 and above appear to be at the highest risk of churning.

### 2. Churn by Geography 

- Germany has the highest churn rate (32.44%).
- Spain and France have considerably lower churn rates.
- Regional retention strategies may be required for the German market.

### 3. Churn by Gender 

- Female customers have a noticeably higher churn rate (25.07%) than male customers (16.46%).
- This indicates that female customers are more likely to leave the bank.
- Further analysis is needed to understand the factors contributing to this difference.

### 4. Churn by Number of Products 

- Customers with 4 products have the highest churn rate (100%), followed by customers with 3 products (82.71%).
- Customers with 2 products have the lowest churn rate (7.58%).
- The extremely high churn rate for customers with four products should be interpreted carefully, as this group may be influenced by a relatively small sample size.

### 5. Churn by Credit Score 

- Customers with Poor credit scores have the highest churn rate (23.73%), while customers with Good credit scores have the lowest (19.18%).
- The difference across credit score groups is relatively small, suggesting that credit score alone is not a strong predictor of customer churn.
- Other factors such as age, geography, and customer activity appear to have a greater impact.

### 6. Churn by Active Member

- Inactive customers have a significantly higher churn rate (26.85%) than active customers (14.27%).
- This suggests that customer engagement plays an important role in retention.
- Encouraging customers to actively use banking services may help reduce churn.


## Business Recommendations 

Based on the analysis, the bank can consider the following actions to reduce customer churn:
- Increase customer engagement by encouraging inactive customers to use digital banking services, personalized offers, and loyalty programs.
- Focus on high-risk customer segments, particularly customers over 40 years old, female customers, and customers located in Germany.
- Review customers with multiple products, especially those holding three or four products, to understand why they are leaving despite having a strong relationship with the bank.
- Strengthen customer retention strategies through targeted campaigns, proactive customer support, and personalized financial recommendations.
- Monitor churn continuously using dashboards to identify high-risk segments early and measure the effectiveness of retention initiatives.

## Conclusion 

This project analyzed customer churn using SQL, Excel, and Tableau to identify the key factors associated with customer attrition.
The analysis indicates that customer churn is more common among older customers, inactive members, female customers, customers located in Germany, and customers with multiple banking products.
The interactive Tableau dashboard enables business users to monitor churn across different customer segments and supports data-driven decision-making for customer retention strategies.
